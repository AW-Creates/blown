Three brand directions

Each takes a different angle on the same brief: trust, economic empowerment, and everyday usability. None are final — pick one to push further, or mix elements (e.g. Ledger's palette with OwnBlack's directness).

The Ledger

"Every business, accounted for."

Leans into the economic-empowerment angle — a ledger is literally a record of value and ownership, which doubles as a metaphor for the verification system itself. It reads as established and trustworthy rather than trendy, which matters for a platform asking people to hand over real trust (and real money).

Unambiguous when spoken aloud, easy to say, no punctuation tricks. Trade-off: it's a real English word already used by other brands (mostly finance/accounting), so you'd want to check trademark availability in your category and lock the domain early.

Sable

Sable

"Black, by definition."

Sable is the heraldic term for black — an old word for it, used on coats of arms and family crests to denote nobility and permanence. It sidesteps the literalism of "Black-owned" in the name itself, which gives you more room for elegant, non-on-the-nose visual branding (a single mark, a signet-style seal) rather than needing the concept spelled out every time.

Trade-off: it's less immediately self-explanatory than the other two directions — first-time visitors won't know what it does from the name alone, so your tagline and hero copy have to do more work.

OwnBlack

OwnBlack

"Search. Verify. Own it."

This is the refined version of your original BL.OWN instinct, keeping the wordplay but fixing the pronunciation problem — it reads correctly the first time, in speech, on a business card, or in a lower-third. The checkmark-in-a-frame icon doubles as "verified" and "ownership stamp," which ties directly to your certification feature.

Most direct and contemporary of the three — good fit if you want the app to feel more like a modern consumer product (Yelp/Instagram energy) than an institutional directory. Trade-off: least distinctive of the three names; closer in spirit to existing apps like BlackDollar and BuyBlack, so the visual identity has to carry more of the differentiation.

My lean

The Ledger is the strongest fit for the actual differentiator you're building — verified trust and economic empowerment — and it's the direction the landing page mockup is built around. If you want something that feels faster and more social/consumer-facing instead of institutional, OwnBlack is the safer, more legible evolution of your original idea. I'd avoid deciding on vibes alone — happy to check live domain and basic trademark-class availability for whichever direction you want to pressure-test first.
